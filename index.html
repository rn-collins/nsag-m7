Urban planning is health governance. Every zoning decision, development approval, and infrastructure investment shapes the mental health of the people living in that environment. Most planning processes have no mechanism for governing those effects.

What the module examines

Evans (2003) reviewed the research literature linking built environment to mental health outcomes and established that neighborhood disorder, housing quality, noise, and green space are independent mental health determinants: environmental conditions that produce mental health outcomes rather than simply correlating with them. Sarkar et al. (2018) confirmed the equity dimension in 94,879 UK Biobank participants: the protective effect of residential greenness is strongest in lower-income populations. Münzel et al. (2014) documented the physiological mechanism for noise: cortisol elevation, sleep architecture fragmentation, and cardiovascular stress pathway activation. The populations most harmed by poor built environment governance are those least able to compensate through private means.

M7 sits in the Civic & Built Environment group of the framework.

What the assessment measured

The module organised a structured self-assessment across six governance dimensions:

  1. 1Mental Health Impact Assessment
  2. 2Green Space Standards
  3. 3Noise & Environmental Stressor Management
  4. 4Housing Quality Standards
  5. 5Community Participation in Design
  6. 6Equity Impact Assessment

Each dimension was described against tiers running from early stage up to the fully implemented tier the framework calls PIONEERING, with observable criteria written for each level, so that an institution could locate its own arrangements rather than receive a score. It was a self-assessment framework for institutional reflection, and never a validated instrument, an audit, an accreditation, or a compliance determination.

Who it was written for

Municipal planning departments · Urban design firms · Parks and recreation departments · Housing authorities · Transportation agencies · Public health departments

And anyone who lives in the city being planned. Every zoning, noise, and green-space decision lands on a nervous system, and lands hardest on those least able to move away.

Why the standalone deployment was retired

The fifteen modules were first published as fifteen separate deployments. Scope, evidence, and release status then had to be maintained in fifteen places, and they drifted apart. The hub now holds one canonical page per module, and these fifteen addresses point at it.

Assessment collection is paused across all fifteen modules. The published operations matrix records the same position for every one of them: the canonical route is reachable, collection is paused, and advisory work is delivered by a person rather than by automated scoring. This page is a static record. It carries no forms and collects nothing.
